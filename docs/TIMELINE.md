# Representative MVOD Timeline

This timeline selects works that make a visible change in problem formulation or evidence source. Online-first and proceedings years can differ; the registry stores both when verified.

- **2011–2013 — cluster and cross-view inconsistency:** early work frames multi-view anomalies as inconsistent cluster or affinity behavior across aligned views.
- **2015–2018 — structured representation:** DMOD, multi-view low-rank analysis, consensus-regularized representations, LDSR, and collective partial-view learning formalize attribute/class signals and reduce reliance on raw pairwise distances.
- **2016–2020 — probabilistic latent models:** robust and hierarchical Bayesian models treat anomalous views as poorly explained by one shared latent cause.
- **2019–2022 — local and deep representation:** neighborhood-based MVAD, MODDIS, fast encoders, NCMOD, CGAEs, and local-similarity-preserving self-representation expand nonlinear and local evidence.
- **2023–2024 — disentanglement, graphs, and contrast:** dPoE separates shared and view-specific factors; graph denoising, information-aware learning, shared-latent neural models, diffusion, and partial-view contrastive learning broaden the design space.
- **2025–2026 — filtering, high-order structure, ensembles, and missing-view experts:** verified proceedings and public preprints explore optimal graph filters, low-rank tensor models, spherical neighborhood ensembles, and incomplete-view fusion.

This is not a claim that all later work supersedes earlier baselines. Many recent methods still inherit the early synthetic anomaly protocols, which can make improvements sensitive to corruption and preprocessing choices.
